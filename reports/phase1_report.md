## 1. Executive summary

Independent resampling produces a correct alternative often enough to matter:
when the first Biomni trajectory is wrong, another of four is right **20
percentage points** of the time more than chance alone would suggest (Oracle@4
0.620 vs first-trajectory 0.420 — headroom clears the pre-registered go
threshold by 4x). Simple **self-consistency beats picking the first answer**:
plurality voting reaches 0.580 vs 0.420 for first, a paired improvement of
**+0.16** whose 95% CI `[+0.06, +0.26]` excludes zero. Trajectory-level
agreement (AUROC 0.874) and plurality fraction (0.812) are the two strongest
uncertainty signals measured — clearly stronger than verbalized confidence
(0.789), which is itself usefully discriminative despite being **badly
miscalibrated** (mean stated confidence 0.96 vs actual accuracy 0.59 among
parseable trajectories — an overconfidence gap of 0.37). Trace length is
**inversely** related to correctness (AUROC 0.20–0.23, i.e. flipped ≈0.78–0.80):
longer trajectories in this pilot more often failed than succeeded, the
opposite of the "longer = more thorough" hypothesis and consistent instead
with longer trajectories being ones where the agent struggled and kept
retrying. The confidence-elicitation prompt did not measurably damage task
performance (paired difference +0.06, CI `[-0.10, +0.22]`, not significant).

The most consequential infrastructure finding is a **24% context-overflow
rate** (60/250 planned runs): trajectories that ran long enough to exceed the
served 65,536-token context were terminated by the endpoint before producing an
answer. This is real agent behavior interacting with a real serving constraint,
not a scoring artifact, and it is the dominant cause of incomplete data — see
§5 and §15.

**Go/No-Go: GO**, on criterion A (oracle headroom) and criterion B (usable
signal), both cleared with margin. See §16.

## 3. Methods

Full detail: `reports/phase1_protocol.md` (frozen before the pilot) and
`DECISIONS.md`. Summary:

* **Agent.** Biomni `A1` at commit `400c1f36…`, v0.0.8, unmodified. All
  instrumentation is adapter-based: a LangChain callback on the agent's LLM, a
  patch of `run_with_timeout` (the graph's single code-execution choke point),
  and a wrap of the tool retriever. `patches/` is empty.
* **Model.** `biomni/Biomni-R0-32B-Preview` @ `71432eb3…`, served locally with
  SGLang 0.5.16, TP2, **bfloat16** (the weights ship FP32), context 65,536 via
  the model card's YaRN override at factor 1.0. No proprietary API was called
  — verified per run in `llm_components.json`.
* **Conditions.** A = 1 unmodified trajectory/instance (n=50). B = 4
  trajectories with a final confidence request (n=200). Task prompt
  byte-identical between conditions; the elicitation lives in the system
  prompt.
* **Sampling.** temperature 0.7 (Biomni's own default), max_tokens 8192. Seeds
  were requested and recorded, but the endpoint does **not** honour them
  (`seed_supported: false`) — trajectories are independent samples, not
  reproducible ones.
* **Evaluation.** The official `BiomniEval1._compute_reward`, unmodified. A
  task-aware canonicalization layer converts a raw response into the parsed
  answer that scorer expects; `strict_reward` on the un-normalized token is
  reported alongside (mean 0.527 vs normalized mean 0.574 among completed
  instrumented trajectories — normalization recovered credit on ~4.7% of them,
  chiefly rsID case and OMIM-id-as-int).
* **Statistics.** Resampling unit is the **task instance**; 2,000 bootstrap
  replicates, seed 20260731; cluster bootstrap for trajectory-level
  associations; `GroupKFold` for the exploratory learned selector.
* **Execution.** Two GPUs (not four — logged as a protocol deviation), one TP2
  replica, dispatcher concurrency 8 (raised from 4 mid-run once measured
  throughput showed the allocation would not otherwise finish; also logged).
  Neither change touched prompts, conditions, sampling settings or seeds.

### Mid-pilot bug fixes (both applied, then the pipeline was fully re-run — not patched over)

Two real bugs were found by inspecting the actual pilot data, not by the
smoke test, and both are load-bearing enough that they are documented here
rather than only in `DECISIONS.md`:

1. **Canonicalization gap.** Biomni states its gene-symbol answer
   symbol-first far more often than label-first — e.g. *"**PDGFRB** is
   identified as the most likely causal gene…"* rather than *"answer:
   PDGFRB."* The original canonicalizer only matched the label-first form and
   left 32 trajectories (of 246 present) marked `ambiguous`, all in the three
   `gwas_causal_gene_*` tasks (44–52% of those tasks' trajectories). Added a
   symbol-first-conclusion pattern (`canonicalization.py`); reparsed every
   stored raw response (no model calls needed — the text was already on disk);
   31 of 32 resolved to a clean answer, 1 remains genuinely ambiguous. This
   changed every headline number: first-trajectory reward moved 0.36 → 0.42,
   plurality 0.50 → 0.58, oracle headroom 24 pp → 20 pp, confidence AUROC
   0.805 → 0.789, confidence accuracy (among parseable) 0.51 → 0.59. The
   **numbers in this report are post-fix**; see `scripts/reparse_pilot.py` for
   the exact repair procedure.
2. **Context-overflow misclassification.** The endpoint uses two different 400
   error phrasings for context overflow (`"maximum context length ... exceeds"`
   vs `"the input (N tokens) is longer than the model's context length"`); the
   classifier only recognised the first, so 2 genuine overflow failures were
   mislabelled `unknown_failure`. Fixed and relabelled from the recorded error
   text — no reparsing of raw responses needed for this one.

Both fixes are regression-tested (`tests/test_canonicalization.py`,
`tests/test_resumption.py`) and the run manifest, sampling and conditions are
unchanged — only the interpretation of already-collected raw responses was
corrected.

**Context overflow is the dominant failure mode**, and it is not evenly
distributed: `rare_disease_diagnosis` (13/25, 52%), `patient_gene_detection`
(11/25, 44%), `crispr_delivery` (9/25, 36%) and `lab_bench_seqqa` (9/25, 36%)
carry most of it; `gwas_causal_gene_opentargets` and
`gwas_variant_prioritization` had **zero**. Overflowing trajectories ran 3.2×
longer on average before failing — consistent with an agent that gets stuck in
a long retry/re-planning loop and eventually exhausts the context window rather
than one that overflows immediately. This is a genuine agent behavior
interacting with a genuine serving limit (see `DECISIONS.md` D-04), not a
configuration mistake: the context ceiling was already raised from the native
40,960 to 65,536 before any run, precisely because the system prompt alone
can consume 17k–41k tokens.

**Is this "infrastructure failures dominate biological reasoning failures"**
— the pre-registered stop criterion? Judged **no**: 75.2% of trajectories
completed, and among those, 38% of instances got all four trajectories wrong
and 80% showed some disagreement — real reasoning variance, not an artifact of
missing data. But 24% context overflow is high enough to be a **first-priority
engineering fix for Phase 2** (larger context budget, or a step/token budget
that forces an earlier answer), not a footnote.

The full Biomni E1 bioinformatics environment was not installed (by design,
documented in advance); commonly-imported tool dependencies were added after
the smoke test exposed gaps. Residual tool failures are visible in the data
(`failed_tool_call_count` > 0 on many trajectories, §11) but were not the
dominant failure mode.

**No significant effect** of the confidence request on task performance,
output length, tool use, or wall time — every CI comfortably contains zero.

The 54% answer-change rate is real but **cannot be cleanly attributed to the
perturbation**: sampling is unseeded (§15), so trajectory 0 under Condition A
and trajectory 0 under Condition B are two independent stochastic samples of
the same prompt, not a controlled A/B pair. The methodologically clean
finding is the *reward* comparison (no effect); the answer-change rate is
reported for completeness but is confounded with ordinary sampling variance,
which the RQ2/RQ1 results (80% disagreement even *within* one condition) show
is already substantial.

genuinely producing different answers, which is the precondition for any
selection method to help at all.

Oracle is an **upper bound that reads ground truth**. It is never presented
as a usable method.

## 10. Confidence calibration (RQ3)

| quantity | value |
| --- | --- |
| Trajectories requesting confidence | 200 |
| Valid confidence parsed | 117 (58.2% of 201 requesting; 2 additional requests are counted `absent` due to missing runs) |
| Mean stated confidence | 0.96 |
| Accuracy (among parseable) | 0.59 |
| **Overconfidence gap** | **0.37** |
| Brier score | 0.367 |
| ECE (5 equal-width bins) | 0.370 |
| AUROC | 0.789, CI [0.690, 0.873] |
| AUPRC | 0.817 |

The reliability diagram (figure `04`) shows confidence clustered almost
entirely in two bins near 0.75 and 0.96 — the model rarely states a low
confidence at all, so calibration is essentially untestable below ~0.7.
**The split result is the finding**: confidence is close to useless as a
calibrated probability (a stated 96% is right barely more than half the time)
but retains real *ranking* value (AUROC 0.789) — correct trajectories are
stated more confidently than incorrect ones, even though the absolute numbers
cannot be trusted. This is exactly the pattern that motivates `max_confidence`
as a selector while also motivating recalibration before using confidence as a
probability in any downstream decision.

Missing-confidence rate (41.8% of runs that requested it) is itself a finding,
not swept aside: it correlates with `model_context_overflow` (the request was
never answered) far more than with a malformed block once the model did
answer (`confidence_parse_failure`, n=17, was rare relative to `missing`,
n=82).

**Length and effort signals are inverted, not merely uninformative.** Flipped
AUROCs of ~0.78–0.80 for token counts and call counts mean *longer, more

trajectories ran 3.2× longer before failing. `failed_tool_call_count` points
the same direction, weakly. The one exception, `visible_plan_step_count`
(AUROC 0.637), is a milder positive signal and is the only individually
weak-but-real non-consistency behavioural signal in this pilot.

for the trajectory-level distributions.

| srlm_style | 0.580 | [0.440, 0.700] | +0.16, p≈0.001 |
| rank_combination | 0.580 | [0.440, 0.700] | +0.16, p≈0.001 |
| plurality | 0.580 | [0.440, 0.700] | +0.16, p≈0.001 |
| plurality_then_confidence | 0.580 | [0.440, 0.700] | +0.16, p≈0.001 |
| plurality_then_shortest | 0.580 | [0.440, 0.700] | +0.16, p≈0.001 |
| min_length | 0.520 | [0.380, 0.660] | +0.10, p≈0.116 |
| max_confidence | 0.520 | [0.380, 0.660] | +0.10, p≈0.121 |
| **first** | 0.420 | [0.280, 0.560] | — |
| random_expected | 0.425 | [0.315, 0.530] | ≈0 |

**Every plurality-anchored selector ties for the best deployable result**
(0.580) and clears `first` with a CI excluding zero. Selectors that ignore
consensus and use only confidence or length (`max_confidence`, `min_length`)
improve over `first` numerically but the paired CI still contains zero
(p≈0.12) — with only 50 instances, these do not reach the same confirmatory
strength as the plurality-based selectors. Restricting to the plurality
cluster before applying confidence or length (`plurality_then_confidence`,
`plurality_then_shortest`, `srlm_style`, `rank_combination`) reproduces plain
`plurality` exactly at this sample size — the secondary tie-break rarely
changes the winner once the majority cluster is already fixed.

**Exploratory learned selector** (grouped 5-fold CV, L2 logistic regression on
confidence, agreement, log-length, call counts): out-of-fold AUROC 0.870,
AUPRC 0.810 — comparable to `agreement_fraction` alone, consistent with
agreement being the dominant signal the model would need to learn. 83/200
`final_confidence` values were median-imputed for this model (the missing-data
rate from §10). Reported as exploratory only; not a claim about a deployable
selector at this sample size.

## 14. Failure examples

**Confidently wrong** (`gwas_causal_gene_opentargets`, run
`gwas_causal_gene_opentargets-i0556-inst-t1-4602e0ca`): stated confidence
0.9+ while the canonical answer (`SLC2A9`, recovered by the symbol-first fix)
scored 0 — one instance where all three of that instance's parseable
trajectories converged on the same wrong answer with high confidence
(`plurality_wrong` without a correct minority to rescue it).

**Plurality rescued a wrong first answer** (2 of 50 instances went the other
way: `p_plurality_wrong_minority_right = 0.04`) — the general pattern favors
plurality, but it is not infallible; see `results/tables/selectors__selection_detail.csv`
for the two counter-examples by `run_id`.

**Context-overflow cascade**
(`rare_disease_diagnosis`, 13/25 trajectories): mean wall time before failure
1507 s vs 468 s for completed trajectories in the same task family —
consistent with a retry loop that never converges rather than a single
oversized query.

**Tool-failure with a correct answer anyway**: several trajectories with
`failed_tool_call_count > 0` still scored 1.0 — Biomni's agent can route
around a failed tool call and still land on the right answer, which is why
`failed_tool_call_count` alone is only weakly predictive (AUROC 0.360, i.e.
flipped ≈0.64) rather than a hard failure signal.

Full traces for every example above are preserved under
`runs/phase1/runs/<task>/i<instance>/<condition>/t<k>/` (`final_response.txt`,
`transcript.json`, `events.jsonl`) and are citable by `run_id`.

## 15. Limitations

1. **50 instances, 250 trajectories.** A pilot. One instance moves an
   instance-level rate by 2 pp; per-task cells hold 5 instances — §13 numbers
   are descriptive, not inferential.
2. **One agent, one model.** No claim of transfer.
3. **Final-answer correctness only.** A correct answer reached through an
   invalid workflow scores identically to a sound one. Workflow validity is
   **not** assessed here — that needs the expert annotation deferred to Phase
   2. Nothing in this report licenses a claim about workflow validity.
4. **Final-only confidence.** Per-step confidence is architecturally
   unavailable in Biomni (`DECISIONS.md` D-08); the SRLM-style selector is an
   approximation.
5. **Seeds requested but not honoured.** Sampling is stochastic and unseeded
   — this weakens the prompt-perturbation comparison specifically (§7) since
   trajectory 0 differs between conditions by both the perturbation *and*
   ordinary sampling noise, with no way to separate the two from this design.
6. **24% context-overflow rate.** The single largest data-completeness issue
   in this pilot (§5). Concentrated in specific tasks (`rare_disease_diagnosis`
   52%, `patient_gene_detection` 44%); absent in others (0% in two GWAS
   tasks). Any Phase-2 design must budget for this explicitly — either a
   larger served context, an explicit step/token cap that forces an earlier
   answer, or both.
7. **Lightweight tool environment.** The full Biomni E1 environment (>10 h,
   >30 GB) was not installed. Commonly-imported dependencies were added after
   the smoke test exposed gaps; residual tool failures are visible in the
   data but were not the dominant failure mode.
8. **Live external databases.** Results can depend on when a trajectory ran.
9. **Context extrapolation** above 40,960 positions (the native ceiling) up to
   the served 65,536 — positions in that range were reached by extrapolation
   the model was not trained on.
10. **`patient_gene_detection` rewards any set intersection**, so a large
    predicted gene set inflates reward; not separately audited in this report
    beyond noting the metric's known permissiveness.
11. **rsID normalization** grants credit a literal comparison would not:
    quantified directly — mean `reward` 0.574 vs `strict_reward` 0.527 among
    completed instrumented trajectories, a 4.7-pp gap attributable to
    normalization credit (chiefly rsID case, OMIM-id-as-int).
12. **Confidence is almost never stated below ~0.7**, so the calibration curve
    is only informative in the high-confidence region; a genuine low-range
    calibration claim cannot be made from this data.
13. **Two mid-pilot canonicalization fixes were required and applied
    retroactively** to already-collected raw responses (§3). This is

## 16. Go / no-go recommendation

**GO — proceed to Phase 2.**

Adjudicated against the pre-specified criteria in
`reports/phase1_protocol.md` §8, fixed before the pilot ran:

* **Criterion A (oracle headroom), CLEARED with margin.** Oracle@4 exceeds
  first-trajectory by 20.0 pp (threshold: ≥5 pp) and relative error-reduction
  potential is 34.5% (threshold: ≥15%).
* **Criterion B (usable signal), CLEARED with margin.** `agreement_fraction`
  (AUROC 0.874, CI entirely above 0.65) and `final_confidence` (AUROC 0.789,
  CI entirely above 0.65) both discriminate correctness well beyond the
  threshold. The paired improvement of `plurality` over `first` (+0.16, CI
  excludes zero) is itself a practically meaningful, statistically clean
  selector win.
* **Criterion C (failure detection)**, plausibly supported but not the
  headline result: `failed_tool_call_count` weakly flags incorrectness
  (flipped AUROC ≈0.64), and context-overflow trajectories are identifiable
  in real time by their anomalous wall time before ever finishing.
* **Criterion D (task-specific effect)**, clearly present: `lab_bench_seqqa`
  shows 60 pp of headroom nearly fully captured by plurality;
  `gwas_variant_prioritization` needs no help at all.

None of the stop criteria triggered: oracle headroom is not negligible;
trajectories disagree 80% of the time (not repeating the same wrong answer);
confidence is miscalibrated but *not* non-predictive (the stop criterion is a
conjunction, and only half held); elicitation did not damage performance; and
while context overflow (24%) is a serious and first-priority infrastructure
issue for Phase 2, it did not dominate over genuine reasoning-failure signal —
188 completed trajectories still show strong, interpretable, statistically
supported findings.

**Separating the four failure types the protocol requires:**

* **Candidate-generation failure**: partial. `crispr_delivery` (headroom 0)
  shows independent sampling failing to generate a correct alternative;
  most other tasks do not.
* **Trajectory-selection failure**: minimal. Plurality captures nearly all of
  the oracle's ceiling by K=2–3 on tasks where headroom exists.
* **Uncertainty-estimation failure**: partial. Agreement and confidence both
  discriminate well; confidence's *calibration* specifically fails.
* **Infrastructure failure**: substantial and specific (context overflow,
  §5, §15) — the clearest engineering priority for Phase 2, addressed before
  scaling sample size.
